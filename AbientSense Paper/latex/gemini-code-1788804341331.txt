\begin{abstract}
Device-free sensing can enable pedestrian and vehicle monitoring without requiring users or targets to carry dedicated devices. However, existing approaches often rely on radar arrays, synchronized infrastructure, or cameras, increasing deployment cost and raising privacy concerns. This paper presents AmbientSense, a zero-infrastructure sensing framework that transforms the Bluetooth Low Energy and dual-band Wi-Fi radios available in commodity smartphones into a passive radio-frequency sensing platform. AmbientSense fuses signal attenuation changes across multiple carrier bands to detect non-transmitting pedestrians and vehicles without target cooperation. To improve robustness in dynamic environments, the framework compares periodic scan epochs with stable, median-filtered signal baselines and applies an explicit noise-floor threshold to distinguish reliable distance estimates from ambiguous measurements. Multiple smartphones can additionally form a cooperative sensing mesh, where cross-node agreement extends sensing coverage and enables collaborative event confirmation. A gamification layer incentivizes reliable participation in this mesh by assigning experience points and confidence scores based on measurement agreement, multi-band evidence, and proximity to the group consensus, linking user rewards to sensing quality rather than arbitrary activity. The framework and its parameters were evaluated using a model-based Monte Carlo harness that replays complete synthetic scenes through the production signal-processing pipeline. Results indicate that a single smartphone detects presence at about 16 m and identifies crossing entities at about 12 m, while cooperative operation extends detection to 20–40 m. The system also reports uncertainty bands when measurements do not support reliable point estimates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Background and Motivation}
Passive RF sensing exploits the fact that the propagation of wireless
signals is modified by physical objects. When a human body or a vehicle
crosses a radio link, it attenuates, scatters and diffracts the
electromagnetic field, and the received signal strength dips and recovers
with a characteristic signature. Because the observer requires no device
attached to the target, this paradigm is called device-free sensing, and it
underpins applications ranging from indoor localization and
ambient-assisted living to occupancy analytics \cite{wilson2010}. The
underlying physics is well understood: shadowing of a pedestrian at
2.4~GHz can reach several decibels with a lateral profile on the order of a
wavelength, and both the depth and the temporal texture of the attenuation
encode information about the entity that caused it~\cite{januszkiewicz2018}.
Modern systems build on statistical inference over RSSI or
channel-state-information (CSI) sequences, spanning radio tomographic
imaging (RTI) and its learned extensions~\cite{alippi2016,li2024,wu2024,cnnrti2025}
as well as activity-recognition pipelines that classify the motion
itself~\cite{wong2024,sanchez2020}.

A second observation is that smartphones already contain the radios such
sensing requires. A modern handset carries a BLE transceiver and a dual-band
(2.4~GHz and 5~GHz) Wi-Fi interface, and can observe the received signal
strength of every advertising beacon and access point (AP) in range
\emph{without} connecting to it. This converts the phone from a
communication terminal into a multi-band passive sensor, where each observed
link (phone--beacon or phone--AP) becomes a measurement ray whose
attenuation state can be monitored in real time. Beacon-level RSSI from
unmodified receivers has been shown to be usable as a sensing
signal~\cite{ramirez2021,milano2024,bai2020}, relocating the sensing problem
from dedicated node deployments~\cite{wilson2010,alippi2016} to software.

A third observation is economic: dedicated sensing hardware is costly, and
camera-based alternatives raise privacy concerns~\cite{khan2023,zhou2020,dmip2023}.
Smartphone-based passive sensing can be deployed at near-zero marginal cost
by people who already carry the device, and when several phones in an area
cooperate, an \emph{AmbientSense mesh}, the geometric diversity of
their measurement rays can increase both accuracy and spatial
reach~\cite{li2024}. Mobile-crowdsensing research shows that such
cooperation must be engineered: participants contribute when mechanisms are
transparent, rewarded, and socially engaging~\cite{zhang2022,wang2018}.
Gamification is therefore treated here as incentive infrastructure rather
than decoration, at the cost of making the \emph{trustworthiness} of
contributed measurements an explicit engineering concern.

The gap addressed here is between what passive RF sensing demonstrably
achieves in the laboratory and what a single, unmodified smartphone held by
an ordinary user can deliver in the field. Three difficulties compound.
First, detection is physics-limited: occlusion attenuation decays with
distance while environmental fluctuation grows with range, so beyond some
distance the event becomes statistically indistinguishable from noise; a
system that does not quantify this limit misleads its user. Second, RSSI
range estimation is notoriously ambiguous; the log-distance model that
converts decibels into meters is, on weak links, extremely ill-conditioned,
and an unconstrained estimate can be wrong by tens of
metres~\cite{ramirez2021,milano2024}. Third, cooperative mode introduces an
integrity problem: a device can report forged beacons or forged
measurements, and the mesh mean, the very quantity that gamified
reliability rewards, becomes an attack
surface~\cite{casar2022,antonioli2020}. Prior work frequently reports
accuracy without stating the distance at which the signal-to-noise ratio
actually supports it, conflates classification with detection, or assumes
infrastructure (synchronized nodes, CSI-capable chipsets) that phones do not
have.

This paper presents AmbientSense, a native smartphone application that turns commodity phones into cooperative sensing nodes without custom infrastructure, relying solely on the devices' BLE and dual‑band Wi‑Fi radios. The application targets scenarios where traditional sensing infrastructure is unavailable, prohibitively expensive to deploy, or infeasible for privacy reasons, and we evaluate the system using a model‑based Monte Carlo test harness that replays synthetic scenes through the same signal‑processing pipeline as the production application.

\subsection{Our Contribution}

AmbientSense makes the following contributions, and its primary contribution is the design and implementation of a smartphone-based RF sensing framework that integrates multi-band signal analysis, cooperative sensing, transit counting, uncertainty‑aware detection, and quality‑aware gamification; the key contributions of AmbientSense are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item A \textbf{multivariate, multi-band fusion classifier} that combines per-link attenuation features, including dip depth, duration, texture, variance, prominence, and multi-link coherence, across BLE, Wi-Fi~2.4~GHz, and Wi-Fi~5~GHz to detect and distinguish pedestrian and vehicle events. We report performance separately by band to expose differences that pooled results would otherwise hide.

\item \textbf{A multivariate, multi‑band fusion classifier} that aggregates attenuation features from individual RF links, including dip depth, duration, texture, variance, prominence, and coherence across links, and measurements from BLE, Wi‑Fi 2.4 GHz, and Wi‑Fi 5GHz are used to detect and distinguish pedestrian and vehicle events, with results reported separately for each band so that band‑specific differences are not obscured by pooled performance figures.

\item A \textbf{scan-epoch change-detection mechanism} based on the log‑distance path‑loss model; the system produces a distance estimate only when the received signal is above a defined noise floor, and when the signal does not satisfy this condition the application reports a radial range band instead of an unsupported point estimate, thereby making the uncertainty in weak‑signal conditions visible to the user.

\item A \textbf{two-node transit counting mechanism} in which two smartphones form a virtual counting gate. Sequential observations of the same crossing event allow AmbientSense to infer transit direction and count pedestrian or vehicle passages without cameras, dedicated counters, or target cooperation. The configuration can be deployed at entrances, corridors, walkways, road segments, crossings, and checkpoints.

\item A \textbf{cooperative sensing mesh} in which multiple smartphones confirm presence and crossing events through cross-node agreement. This extends effective sensing coverage beyond that of an individual phone and combines spatially distributed observations into a shared sensing view.

\item A \textbf{quality-aware gamification mechanism} to translate sensing participation and measurement agreement into real-time confidence scores and experience-point (XP) rewards, and it prefers multi-band evidence, mesh-confirmed events, and measurements close to the mesh consensus, thereby directly tying participant incentives to sensing quality and motivating users to contribute reliable measurements and stay active in the distributed sensing mesh.
\end{itemize}

Four (4) representative \textbf{application scenarios} demonstrate how these mechanisms can be used in practice. First, \textbf{two-node pedestrian and vehicle counting} enables lightweight flow monitoring at entrances, corridors, crossings, and checkpoints using two ordinary smartphones as virtual counting gates. Second, \textbf{urban crowd and pedestrian-flow monitoring} allows participating smartphones to collectively estimate presence, movement, and crowd concentration in public squares, transport hubs, shopping districts, festivals, and other high-density environments, with coverage emerging from device participation rather than dedicated sensor deployment. Third, \textbf{smart mobility and traffic awareness} uses distributed smartphones and two-node counting configurations to provide infrastructure-light information about pedestrian and vehicle flows, crossing events, and movement direction around roads, transport stations, and temporary traffic-management areas. Fourth, \textbf{emergency and disaster situational awareness} enables optimized sensing when fixed infrastructure is damaged or unavailable, providing coarse information about human presence and movement and supporting monitoring of evacuation routes, temporary shelters, entrances, and restricted areas.

As a result, the detection, counting, and cooperative sensing capabilities can support monitoring of pedestrian and vehicle flows, crowd presence and movement, temporary access points, and human activity in environments where dedicated sensing infrastructure is unavailable, disrupted, costly, or difficult to deploy.

\subsection{Related Work}
\textbf{Device-free sensing and radio tomography.} RTI reconstructs an
attenuation field from link measurements over a node
network~\cite{wilson2010}; later work removes the need for training data,
recovers attenuation images generatively, and extends to multi-target and
outdoor scenes~\cite{alippi2016,wu2024}. RTI has since been integrated with
reconfigurable intelligent surfaces to roughly halve the required node
density~\cite{li2024}, and transformer/latent-variable successors improve
robustness under composite noise~\cite{wu2024}. CNN-based reconstruction from
Wi-Fi RSSI on ESP32 meshes reports over 92\,\% localization accuracy in
controlled geometry~\cite{cnnrti2025}, a useful upper bound for what
RTI-style inversion can reach with known node geometry, and a reminder that
phone-held sensing lacks exactly that controlled geometry.

\textbf{Activity recognition from Wi-Fi.} CSI-based classifiers and recent
high-dimensional, multi-transceiver datasets have widened the range of
recognizable actions~\cite{wong2024}, and fusing CSI amplitude with RSSI from
a single dual-band AP has achieved sub-meter indoor
localization~\cite{sanchez2020}. These systems typically assume CSI
extraction or controlled capture hardware and target \emph{deliberately
performed} activities; AmbientSense instead targets opportunistic detection
of people passing by, where only link-level RSSI is observable.

\textbf{Counting and flow estimation.} Device-free counting has been
demonstrated with CSI and deep networks~\cite{khan2023}, Doppler-based flow
estimation~\cite{zhou2020}, and commodity Wi-Fi counting/identification of
moving persons~\cite{dmip2023}. The common trajectory in this strand is the
move from counting toward flow; the two-node transit counter here realizes
the same barrier-coverage geometry on commodity Bluetooth, without dedicated
infrastructure.

\textbf{Ranging and shadowing physics.} RSSI ranging requires integrity
monitoring against unstable beacons and missing
links~\cite{ramirez2021,bai2020}, and human-body shadowing at 2.4~GHz has
been characterized and shown to be compensable in wearable and
sensor-network settings~\cite{januszkiewicz2018}. Fisher-information analysis
of device-free localization in integrated sensing-and-communication networks
shows that positional information along a link collapses beyond a certain
SNR~\cite{li2024}, which supports the decision below to report distance
\emph{bands} rather than points at long range.

\textbf{Incentives and data integrity.} Quality-weighted and reputation-based
incentive design outperforms raw-volume rewards in mobile
crowdsensing~\cite{zhang2022,wang2018}. On the adversary side, BLE security
has been surveyed comprehensively~\cite{casar2022}, downgrade attacks on key
negotiation are known~\cite{antonioli2020}, and physical-layer fingerprints
rather than identifiers can support spoofing
detection~\cite{cai2024,mobisys2024,hensler2019}. These results bound what a
future adversarial-resilience module for the AmbientSense mesh would need
to defend against; the present mesh design (Section~II.H) provides
majority-agreement weighting but does not yet implement such a defense, and
we return to this in Section~IV.

\subsection{Paper Organization}
Section~II presents the system design: the sensing model, the DSP chain,
multivariate fusion, ranging and gating, scan epochs, mesh agreement, the
two-node counter, gamification, and the privacy boundary. Section~III
reports the evaluation harness and the per-radius accuracy, epoch-detection,
ranging, mesh, and transit results drawn from it. Section~IV concludes and
outlines future work, including the on-device validation and adversarial
hardening that the current results do not yet cover.

\section{System Design and Architecture}
AmbientSense is a device-free sensing architecture that exploits signal
perturbations in commodity BLE and Wi-Fi links to detect nearby movement.
The system combines heterogeneous radio observations with local signal
processing, event-level inference, and distributed multi-device fusion.
Figure \ref{fig:method} summarizes the architecture and the functional dataflow of AmbientSense.

\subsection{Functional Architecture}

The \emph{Hardware} layer provides BLE and Wi-Fi observations using
the standard smartphone radios. The \emph{Per-Link DSP} layer performs local
signal conditioning and event detection.

The \emph{Inference} layer converts radio perturbations into semantic sensing
results, including entity classification, confidence, and range information.
The \emph{Application} layer manages events, epochs, and transit-level
operations. At the highest level, \emph{Gamification and Mesh} enables
multiple devices to exchange aggregate observations, establish agreement,
estimate reliability, and provide participation-based feedback. As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:method}, the sensing pipeline consists of four
main stages. First, BLE and Wi-Fi radio front-ends provide link-level RSSI
observations. These observations are processed independently using adaptive
signal windows, baseline estimation, and event detection to identify
significant signal perturbations. Second, detected events are transformed into a compact set of temporal and
signal-strength features, including attenuation, duration, signal texture,
and cross-link coherence. These features are provided to the
\texttt{EntityClassifier}, which performs asymmetric multi-band fusion to
classify the observed event as a pedestrian or vehicle.

\begin{figure}[htb!]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=8.7cm]{figures/fig_modern_01_architecture.jpg}
  \caption{Functional architecture of AmbientSense. Heterogeneous BLE and
  Wi-Fi observations are transformed through local signal processing,
  feature extraction, and multi-band inference into spatial, temporal, and
  mesh-level sensing artifacts.}
  \label{fig:method}
\end{figure}

Third, the system derives spatial and temporal information from the detected
events. Depending on signal quality, ranging is represented either as a
distance estimate or an uncertainty band, while scan epochs provide a
temporal representation of changes in the observed radio environment.

Finally, the resulting sensing information is converted into higher-level
artifacts, including event records, range estimates, epoch snapshots, and
mesh records. These artifacts provide the interface to the evaluation
harness and to the distributed sensing layer.

\subsection{Distributed Sensing and Privacy}

Multiple AmbientSense devices can cooperate as a sensing mesh. Devices
exchange derived crossing records rather than raw radio measurements, and
multiple observations can be combined to improve confidence and reliability.
The gamification layer uses the same agreement information to reward
participation and multi-node confirmation. The architecture follows a device-free and privacy-oriented model. No
camera or microphone is required, radio-level measurements remain local to
the device, and we do not retain persistent beacon identifiers. Only
aggregate sensing events are shared with the mesh when distributed operation
is enabled.

The fusion process applies an agreement tolerance of±1 crossing or ±4m, and assigns a × 1.25 reliability factor to nodes whose observations are consistent with the group estimate, while for applications requiring perimeter monitoring or directional tracking, the general mesh can be replaced by a transit mode in which two nodes form a digital tripwire; this mode analyzes disruptions and re‑establishment of Classic Bluetooth and BLE connections to detect passing objects and estimate their size and speed, and combining the two protocols makes the technique less susceptible to noise than using either protocol alone.

Sustaining a large-scale, crowdsourced sensor network requires active user participation and continuous device uptime. To encourage users to leave the software running and participate in multi-node meshes, the deployment relies heavily on gamification techniques.

% III. EVALUATION
%---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Evaluation}

Seven quantities are measured against injected ground truth: (A) per-crossing
sensing accuracy and detection versus distance and radius preset; (B)
scan-epoch movement detection and false-positive rate; (C) ranging coverage
and median absolute error for gated versus ungated estimators; (D) mesh
confirmation and false-confirmation rates; (E) transit-counter detection and
typing versus phone separation; (F) per-band and fused typing accuracy; and
(G) displayed gamified accuracy versus realized accuracy.

All metrics are extracted from a unified, model-based Monte Carlo harness
(1,512 operational scenes and 160 empty control scenes for the main sensing
and mesh experiments, with dedicated sub-experiments of 288 scenes for
ranging and 56 control epochs for change detection, detailed below). The
harness synthesizes radioscape dynamics by combining log-distance path loss,
log-normal scintillation, and Gaussian-profile shadow fading governed by
Fresnel-efficiency decay:
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{rx}}(d) = P_0 - 10n \log_{10}\left(\frac{d}{d_0}\right) - A_0 \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta x^2}{2\sigma^2}\right) \eta_{\mathrm{Fresnel}} + X_\sigma
\label{eq:pathloss}
\end{equation}
Candidate crossings inject explicit entity parameters (pedestrians with
$\sigma = 0.35$~m; vehicles with $\sigma = 1.15$~m, occlusion capped at
34~dB, 5.5~Hz flutter), trajectory vectors, and closest-approach distances
(0.5--20~m) across near (10~m), far (55~m), and mixed AP baselines (33
typical links spanning 12--68~m legs).

Because ground truth is known exactly, accuracy, detection, error, and
false-positive rates are directly measurable, and empty/control scenes are
generated in parallel so that false-change and false-confirmation rates are
measured with denominators rather than assumed to be low. Every candidate
change to a preset, gate, weight vector, epoch rule, or detector fix was A/B
tested on complete scenes before being accepted, and the same distributions
drive what the shipped app shows as radius presets, range bands, and
accuracy figures.

\subsection{Sensing Accuracy and Multi-Band Fusion}
Single-device BLE detection yields 100\,\% sensitivity at 2~m, drops to
$\approx$50\,\% at 14--16~m, and falls below 15\,\% beyond 20~m; the
auxiliary Wi-Fi bands extend physical leg coverage to 58--68~m
(Table~\ref{tab:bands}).

Per-crossing accuracy across radius presets is shown in
Fig.~\ref{fig:accuracy}. Narrow presets maximize near-field trigger
sensitivity at the expense of field truncation; expansive presets raise the
adaptive trigger threshold to absorb path scintillation over longer
baselines, which blunts sensitivity. The 10-m preset is recommended for default use in standalone operation, and across the entire system and all 1,512 Monte Carlo runs, pedestrians are correctly classified in 34.9\% of occupied scenes and vehicles in 29.6\%, while 87.3\% of unperturbed control scenes are correctly classified as quiescent; moreover, the observed median attenuation dips in Fig.~8 are consistent with expected physical behavior, because as the closest-approach distance increases, shadowing weakens with decreasing Fresnel-zone efficiency until the resulting attenuation reaches the adaptive threshold floor. Reporting performance at discrete sensing radii eliminates the bias that can occur from global aggregation, and, for example, BLE-based entity-typing accuracy falls from 92\% at 2~m to 47\% at 20~m and 6\% at 68~m, but beyond about 20--40~m the link-axial Fisher information effectively collapses \cite{li2024}, therefore, AmbientSense reports spatial range bands rather than point estimates past this interval, reflecting a limitation of the physical radio channel rather than an arbitrary software constraint. In aggregate, a standalone device can reliably classify crossing entities up to about 12~m and detect their presence up to about 16~m; at longer ranges, extended-range Wi-Fi links act mostly as peripheral tripwires rather than sources of fine-grained entity classification.



\subsection{Scan Epochs and Ranging Estimator Gating}
Inter-epoch analysis tracks RSSI between consecutive observation windows.
Under baseline conditions, movement is detected in 100\,\%, 88\,\%, 81\,\%,
75\,\%, 62\,\%, 12\,\%, 44\,\%, and 25\,\% of candidate crossings at true
distances of 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, and 22~m, respectively
(Table~\ref{tab:epoch}), while the average number of perturbed links falls
from 5.0 at 2~m to 0.2 at 22~m.

\subsection{Cooperative Mesh and Two-Node Transit Counting}
Confirmation rates in the mesh scale with receiver density, most visibly at
long range (Table~\ref{tab:mesh}, $N=10$ per cell). At 40~m, confirmation
rises from 20\,\% with a single device to 50\,\% across five cooperating
nodes; at 5~m, from 20\,\% to 100\,\%. The non-monotonic step at 10~m
(70\,\%~$\to$~40\,\%) reflects the overhead of multi-node consensus, where
candidate events must align within $\pm5$~s in matching classification
buckets before a crossing is confirmed. Despite this overhead, the voting
logic produced zero false confirmations across all 160 evaluated empty
scenes (Rule-of-Three 95\,\% upper bound: 1.88\,\%), and distributed
coherence extends effective detection from 4--12~m (single phone) toward
20--40~m with mesh cooperation.

\subsection{Gamified Accuracy and Parameter Selection}
The gamification layer calculates confidence in real-time and converts sensing contributions to a well-defined XP progression, giving each detected crossing a base XP reward; multi-band fusion is given a $2\times$ bonus and mesh-confirmed events an additional $15\text{ XP}$, while nodes with estimates closest to the mesh consensus centroid receive an additional $1.25\times$ multiplier. This reward structure is not an arbitrary points system but mirrors the operational likelihood distribution; therefore, the accuracy estimate shown to users comes from the same underlying distributions used to compute the performance results reported above. We chose all parameters in the deployed system using the complete-scene A/B protocol described in Section~I.C rather than row-level optimization, which produced misleading results.

Automated weight optimization provided a small row-level $F_1$ boost from $0.173$ to $0.179$, but fared poorly in complete-scene simulations: an unconstrained optimizer converged to a degenerate solution with $0\%$ human recall, while a constrained alternative achieved $45.6\%$ scene-level accuracy, compared to $46.7\%$ for the heuristic configuration used in the deployed system. Moreover, the complete-scene protocol also led us to reject uniform-weight multi-band averaging, which reduced BLE accuracy to $31.0\%$ (Section~III.A), noise-floor range extrapolation, which induced phantom offsets of $14$--$23\text{ m}$ (Section~III.B), and naive sample-delta epoch comparison, which resulted in false-positive rates of $25\%$ to $62\%$ (Section~III.B).

\subsection{Limitations and Untested Conditions}
All reported results are model-based and require on-device validation. Within the simulated envelope, AmbientSense identifies crossings to about 12~m and presence to about 16~m, with range bands replacing point estimates beyond about 8~m. Epoch-based change detection achieves zero false positives within the tested 8~m range, while cooperative sensing extends the simulated detection radius to 20--40~m without false confirmations.
%---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% IV. CONCLUSIONS
%---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
AmbientSense demonstrates that commodity smartphones can form a practical, infrastructure-free sensing platform by combining BLE and dual-band Wi-Fi measurements for passive multi-band detection, gated ranging, field-change detection, two-node transit counting, and cooperative mesh sensing. The resulting architecture supports applications ranging from \textbf{two-node pedestrian and vehicle counting} at entrances and corridors, to \textbf{urban crowd and flow monitoring}, \textbf{smart-mobility and roadside awareness}, and \textbf{emergency situational awareness} where dedicated sensing infrastructure may be unavailable. A gamification layer further links participation and rewards to measurement agreement, providing an incentive mechanism for sustaining the distributed sensing mesh.

The resulting model allows sensing coverage and counting capacity to scale with participating devices rather than dedicated infrastructure, while gamification provides an incentive for maintaining the sensing population and cooperative agreement improves confidence as additional observations become available. Finally, AmbientSense explicitly reports the signal conditions, parameter configurations, and operating ranges at which it declines to produce a confident estimate, providing a reproducible characterization of the practical limits of commodity-radio sensing.

Future evaluation will focus on diverse indoor and outdoor environments, device heterogeneity, placement, multipath, interference, and battery constraints. Mesh resilience against spoofed or adversarial reports also remains untested and is left for future work. Future work will also address \textbf{mesh integrity and incentive robustness}, extending the current agreement-based rewards toward reputation- and quality-aware mechanisms that remain reliable under noisy, spoofed, or coordinated device behavior. These extensions will allow AmbientSense to progress from a simulated cooperative sensing framework toward a scalable, gamified mobile sensing infrastructure for real-world deployments.
